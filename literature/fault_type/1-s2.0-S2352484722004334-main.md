1. Introduction
The increasing length and complexity of transmission networks coupled with their harsh operating environment
are susceptible to various fault events that lead to disturbances or interruptions. In order to tackle fault events for
line maintenance and reliable power supply, it is necessary to classify root causes of faults by their signatures.
Transmission line faults may be caused by natural phenomena, creature activities and equipment failures, which
are usually identified through expert analysis based on weather and fault behavior. However, manual diagnosis
basedonexpertknowledge istime-consumingandplacesagreatdemand onpersonnelquality.Withthe increasing
monitoring techniques and advanced recording equipment being implemented to support fault diagnosis, associated
multi-source fault data are too voluminous to interpret manually.

Several studies have been carried out to achieve line fault cause classification using data-driven methods. Rule-
based methods, such as CN2 rule induction [1] and fuzzy logic [2,3], have been applied to identify fault causes
with theintervention ofdomain experts. Dueto the limitationsof thefault scene andthresholds, rule-basemethods
have limited scalability and robustness. Machine learning (ML) methods are expected to classify fault causes
automatically. In [4], the authors combine logistic regression (LR) and artificial neural network (ANN) to classify
animal-caused and tree-caused outages with contextual features. In [5], a cause identifier based on deep belief
network (DBN) is developed and original waveform characteristics are extracted from current traveling wave. The
authorsin[6]providewaveformsignaturesbasedonrootmeansquare(RMS)anddiscretewavelettransform(DWT)
analysis and differentiate four types of cause using decision tree (DT). In [7], authors integrate historical outage,
load and weather data to form a contextual feature set and make classification with ensemble tree models.
As shown in the mentioned literatures, contextual and waveform features are both able to realize a high degree
of accuracy without each other. However, there are high data requirements such as ample field context data and
high-resolution recording signal. In order to develop fault cause classification based on common data condition,
we believe the combined used of contextual and waveform features can improve the accuracy and the challenge of
information fusion need to be addressed. The authors in [8] first differentiate two feature types for comparison and
reveal that feature concatenations even hamper the classification performance. Few studies make a further study on
using both waveform and contextual characteristics for performance improvement.
Tocopewiththischallenge,multi-viewlearningisintroducedtofusecontextualandwaveformfeaturessincethey
describe the same event from different perspectives. In this paper, a multi-view sparse feature selection (MVSFS)
method is proposed for transmission line fault cause classification. Firstly, two-view feature sets are formed and
composed of candidate features that respectively extracted from fault context and waveform. Then, the MVSFS
method is applied to investigate the optimal feature combination. Specifically, ε-dragging is used to enlarge the
margin between classes and a sparse regression model is developed based on l 2,1 -norm penalty terms. Finally, ML
classifiers are employed for final classification with the integrated features. The capability and performance of the
proposed MVSFS are verified by comparative experiments on real-life fault data.

To evaluate the proposed method, 276 line fault samples were collected from the AC network above 110 kV
of a certain city in Guangdong Province, China. Voltage and current signals were recorded by digital fault
recorders (DFRs) and stored in offline files of common format for transient data exchange (COMTRADE). DFRs
recordedfaultsignalswith5kHzsamplingfrequency.Contextinformationwascollectedthroughtheoperationand
maintenance system. Besides data collection for two views, inspection reports of line patrol were attached to these
fault samples and labeled the causes. In terms of root causes, the dataset consisted of 154 lighting, 75 external
force, 28 floating object contact and 19 others. Waveform and contextual features in Table 1 were extracted from
this dataset to form the multi-view feature pools, followed by z-score standardization. After that, the subsequent
